\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Credit-default prediction supports lending decisions, but predictive performance
alone does not show why a model makes a decision or whether it behaves
differently for applicants in different age groups. This thesis evaluates a
LightGBM classifier on the public Credit Risk Dataset after a fairness-oriented
data audit. The cleaned data contain 32,409 loans and 11 recorded predictors.
The study compares SHAP explanations with LightGBM gain and split importance,
measures demographic parity and equal opportunity for applicants under 25 and
those aged 25 and above, retrains the model without age to audit proxy features,
and evaluates reweighing as a mitigation method. The audit found that the
previous pipeline retained 165 duplicate rows, removed every applicant aged 47
or older through z-score filtering, and introduced ratio features that recovered
age exactly. These findings motivated a new preparation procedure based on
logical validation, native missing-value handling, native categorical features,
and stratification by age group and outcome. The final research results should
be reported using the fixed split, 2,000 bootstrap resamples, and ten additional
stratified splits described in this thesis.

\vspace{1cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} credit default prediction, LightGBM, SHAP,
algorithmic fairness, proxy bias, reweighing
